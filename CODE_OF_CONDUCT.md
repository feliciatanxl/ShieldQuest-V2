# Code of Conduct

ShieldQuest is a game about looking out for each other, and we want working on it to feel the same way. This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in the project: maintainers, contributors, reviewers, co-design participants and anyone in our issues, pull requests, discussions or events.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make taking part in ShieldQuest a respectful, welcoming and harassment-free experience for everyone. That holds regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Many people who care about this project are young, including students, youth co-designers and pilot participants. We hold ourselves to the standard you would expect in a youth programme.

## Expected behaviour

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to open source or to coding.
- Give and receive feedback respectfully. Critique the work, not the person.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you conclude.
- Use welcoming, inclusive language.
- Own your mistakes, apologise to those affected, and learn from them.
- Focus on what is best for the young people this game is for.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Insulting, demeaning or discriminatory comments, jokes or imagery.
- Sexualised language or imagery. This includes any content involving minors, which we report to the relevant authorities.
- Sharing anyone's personal information without their explicit permission. This includes real names, schools, photos, phone numbers or NRIC numbers of youth participants.
- **Pressuring anyone to disclose personal experiences** of being scammed, bullied or involved in an offence. ShieldQuest never requires disclosure, and neither do we.
- **Victim-blaming.** Scams and peer pressure are the fault of the people who exploit others, not the people they target.
- Posting real scam links, real phone numbers or working scam scripts. Use fictional examples (`.test` domains, `8XXX XXXX`).
- Any other conduct that would be inappropriate in a school or youth setting.

## Safeguarding

If anything you see in this project suggests a young person may be at risk, tell a maintainer privately and promptly. If someone is in danger right now in Singapore, call **999** (or SMS **70999** if it is not safe to talk). For emotional support, the **National mindline (1771)** and **Samaritans of Singapore (1767)** are available 24/7.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces: the GitHub repository, issues and pull requests, any project chat channels, co-design workshops and pilot sessions. It also applies when someone represents the project in public.

## Reporting

Report concerns privately to the maintainers:

- On GitHub, contact the repository owner [@feliciatanxl](https://github.com/feliciatanxl) privately.
- If you are taking part in a pilot or workshop, you can also tell the facilitator or a teacher from your institution.

Please include what happened, where and when, and any links or screenshots. Remove other people's personal details from screenshots where you can. All reports are handled as confidentially as possible. We will not retaliate against anyone who reports in good faith.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will review every report and respond as quickly as they can. Depending on the situation, responses may include:

1. **Correction:** a private conversation explaining why the behaviour was inappropriate.
2. **Warning:** a formal warning, with clear consequences if it continues.
3. **Temporary ban:** a break from interacting with the project for a set time.
4. **Permanent ban:** removal from all project spaces.

Where a youth participant is involved, maintainers will also follow the safeguarding procedures of the partner institution.
